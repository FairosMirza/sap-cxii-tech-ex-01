## D01 What "similar" means
*21 Oct · Decided*

**Decision.** A similar product is a plausible alternative a shopper might buy *instead of* the query
product. Product type and intended use dominate; style, material, colour, brand and price refine the
ranking. Other brands are eligible. Complements and personalisation are out of scope.

**Why.** The README leaves similarity undefined. A substitute definition makes relevance judgeable by
a person and guides every weight.

**Rejected.** Using `other_items_customers_buy` as ground truth: it mixes complements with
substitutes (the sample record lists a memory card next to kurta fabric) and does not map reliably to IDs.

## D02 Scope and priorities
*21–22 Oct · Decided*

**Decision.** One service covers the original README (similarity function, FastAPI, Docker, ANN bonus,
multimodal bonus) and the v2 specification (embedding fusion, `/semantic_search`, filtered ANN search,
one-page trade-off note). Order: TF-IDF baseline → embeddings → semantic search and filters → API,
Docker → ANN → evaluation → documentation. Image features and semantic IDs are experiments after that.

**Why.** v2 states an effort budget of 5–7 hours: depth belongs in reasoning and validation rather than
breadth. The five-working-day window is the submission deadline, not a licence to add scope.

## D04 Duplicates and variants
*21–22 Oct · Decided*

**Decision.** Exclude the query's own ASIN and return at most one row per ASIN. A *variant family* is the
same brand plus the same normalised title (mostly size variants): the query's family is excluded and at
most one member of any other family is returned. Identical titles **without** a brand are not merged.

**Why.** The 471 duplicate-ASIN rows (451 groups) all have identical titles: true duplicates. In contrast,
five "Juniper Women's Straight Kurta" products have no brand field but different ASINs, prices
(₹599–₹1,036) and images: distinct products. Identical titles alone do not prove duplication.

**Optional rule.** `max_per_title` (experimental value 2) is a result-diversity rule, off for the primary
comparison. It keeps relevance order, fills from lower ranks and records any result shortfall. It will be
compared using human relevance and visual-redundancy judgements before any default is chosen.

**Decision on the cap** (22 Oct, development labels, `reports/dev_evaluation.json`): off by default. With the cap
at 2, C_bge's nDCG@10 changes by −0.001 (95% interval −0.016 to +0.014) and P@5 not at all, while results
repeating an earlier title fall from 1.45 to 0.95 per top-10; for the other systems nDCG moves by at most 0.003
and repeats roughly halve. People judged relevance only; visual redundancy was not judged, and the repeat count
is an automatic proxy. The cap remains available as `max_per_title`.

## D05 Scoring and fusion maths
*21–22 Oct · Decided*

**Decision.** For a query product q and candidate x:

score(q, x) = Σ w·s over components both products have ÷ max(Σ w over those components, 0.5 × Σ all w)

- **Text and image:** cosine of unit vectors, calibrated per representation so that a typical random pair
  maps to 0, a typical best match to 0.9 and an identical item to 1.
- **Price and weight:** exp(−|ln a − ln b| / ln 2). Scale-free (₹50 vs ₹100 scores like ₹500 vs ₹1,000),
  bounded in [0, 1], and halving or doubling gives 1/e. Raw numbers are never mixed with unit vectors.
- **Brand, category:** exact match. **Colour:** Jaccard overlap of colour families.
- **Fixed attribute weights:** category 0.40, price 0.20, brand 0.15, colour 0.15, weight 0.10, rating 0.
  Only the three outer weights (text, image, attributes) are tuned.
- **Ties:** rating, then review count, then product ID.

**Why late fusion (a weighted average of per-component scores) rather than one concatenated vector.**
Each component stays interpretable, and missing features are handled per pair instead of being
zero-filled. **Why calibration.** Representations have different baselines (random bge-small pairs already
score about 0.59 cosine; random TF-IDF pairs about 0); without it, whichever modality has the higher
baseline dominates, and having a feature at all would be rewarded. **Why the 0.5 evidence floor.** It
stops a candidate that matches one cheap field from scoring as if it matched everything.
**Why only three tuned weights.** Twenty development queries cannot reliably tune six to eight weights.

**Cosine vs dot product vs Euclidean.** For unit vectors they rank identically (‖a − b‖² = 2 − 2·cos);
cosine is used because its values are interpretable and it maps directly onto inner-product indexes.
Euclidean distance on raw prices would let price magnitude dominate everything else.

## D06 Product type must dominate
*22 Oct · Decided*

**Finding.** A category weight of 0.40 does not guarantee that type wins: the other attributes total 0.60
and renormalisation shifts their influence. With attributes only (approach A) type loses in three
controlled cases, recorded as strict expected failures in `tests/test_type_dominance.py`:
the right type at double the price; a wrong type with no category (renormalised to a perfect 1.0); the
right type with a wrong category label. With text evidence, all three pass.

**Decision.** No type penalty for now. Revisit only if the labels show the same failure in the chosen
approach. A universal same-category filter is rejected: it would discard valid alternatives and products
with missing or wrong categories.

**Labels (22 Oct).** They do not show the failure in the chosen approach, so the decision stands. Of C_bge's 200
development results, 11 (5.5%) are irrelevant, and only 3 of those come from a different category than the query
(5 share it, 3 have no known category): a type penalty could fix at most 3 of 200. The category signal itself
carries real weight: setting its weight to 0 in C_bge lowers nDCG@10 by 0.128 (95% interval 0.057 to 0.211) and
P@5 by 0.10.

## D07 Evaluation protocol
*21–22 Oct · Decided*

- **Similar-product queries (30):** chosen by stratum with a fixed seed before any model ran
  (`eval/queries.json`): common and rare categories, missing category/brand/price, image failures,
  variant families, ambiguous titles, duplicate ASINs. 20 development, 10 holdout.
- **Search queries (20):** 10 development (the v2 samples plus awkward phrasings) and 10 holdout written
  before any system ran on them (`eval/semantic_queries.json`).
- **Blind pooled labelling:** top-10 of every compared system, de-duplicated and shuffled. System, score
  and extracted category are hidden. Grades: 3 strong substitute, 2 reasonable, 1 weak, 0 irrelevant
  (search: 3 exactly what was asked … 0 irrelevant).
- **Supplemental pools:** when a system change retrieves pairs the pools lack, only those pairs go to a separate,
  shuffled pool (`python -m scripts.build_label_pools --supplement`), judged blind with the same rubric. Existing
  pools are never rewritten; the builder refuses to overwrite them. First use: 3 pairs after the encoder change
  (D17). The labeller knew those pairs came from that change; system, score and twin status are not shown.
- **Label validation:** `python -m scripts.check_labels --rankings` must pass before evaluation. It rejects
  malformed lines, grades outside 0–3, labels for unpooled pairs, missing labels, pairs pooled twice, conflicting
  grades for one pair and any holdout label, and recomputes the pooled systems' current top-10 to confirm that
  every retrieved pair is labelled. Only then does it write `eval/qrels_dev.json`, one grade per query–product
  pair. Before the supplement, the 726 label lines were 726 unique pooled pairs with valid grades, no repeats,
  no conflicts and nothing missing.
- **Metrics:** nDCG@10 and Precision@5 from human labels. Category and brand agreement are diagnostics only.
  Fixed before any metric was computed: nDCG@10 uses gain 2^grade − 1, and the ideal ranking is built from every
  judged product of the query; nDCG with linear gains is reported as a check that the gain choice does not decide
  the comparison. Queries where no judged product has grade ≥ 1 have no ideal ranking: they are listed and left
  out of the means. Precision@5 counts grade ≥ 2 as relevant and always divides by 5, so returning fewer results
  counts against a system. Means come with 95% bootstrap intervals (queries resampled 10,000 times, fixed seed),
  and systems are compared through paired bootstrap differences per query (`python -m scripts.evaluate`).
- **Choosing the approach** (fixed on 22 Oct, after labelling and before any metric was computed): per task, the
  lead is the system with the highest mean nDCG@10. The choice is the least complex system whose paired mean
  difference from the lead is within one standard error (the one-standard-error rule), with complexity ordered in
  advance: A_structured < B_tfidf < C_minilm < C_bge for similar products, TF-IDF < MiniLM < bge-small for
  search. Systems with any unjudged top-10 result are never compared. The holdout is then pooled for the chosen
  system and the TF-IDF baseline only, and reported once; it is not used to choose. Variants (title cap, no
  category) are compared with their base system in the same paired way. The image/no-image slice waits for image
  features (D14): only 1 of the 20 development queries lacks an image.
  *Amendment, 22 Oct, made after seeing the development results:* complexity is judged for the whole service, not
  per task. The per-task order picked C_minilm (0.010 below C_bge, SE 0.023), but search needs bge-small anyway, so
  C_minilm would add a second set of product vectors (44 MB), a second FAISS index (53 MB) and, as built today, a
  second model file (about 90 MB) for no measurable gain. Chosen: C_bge for similar products and bge-small for
  search. Because this was decided after seeing results, the holdout is the unbiased check.
- **Holdout:** pooled only for named finalists and reported once. No approach is chosen before labels exist.
  *Decided 22 Oct, before the holdout was pooled:* it is pooled for C_bge, B_tfidf, bge-small and TF-IDF, plus
  C_minilm and MiniLM as report-only comparisons, so the report can show on unseen queries whether bge-small is
  the better model overall. The choice stays frozen: holdout results cannot change it, and a contradiction would be
  reported, not acted on. From the development overlap, MiniLM adds about 80 judgements (about 410 instead of 330).
- **Latency:** reported as median and p95 with the measured scope stated (e.g. in-process scoring only,
  excluding HTTP and JSON).

## D08 Default search mode rule
*21 Oct · Decided*

FAISS (approximate search) becomes the default only if it reaches at least 0.95 coverage of the exact
hybrid top-10 on the evaluation queries; otherwise exact search is the default. Exact search stays
available as the reference either way.

**Benchmark** (22 Oct, `reports/ann_benchmark.json`, `python -m scripts.benchmark_ann`): in-process, Apple silicon;
the 20 development queries the rule names, plus a seeded sample of 1,000 catalogue products and 300 searches made
from product titles; holdout queries excluded. Coverage is the share of the exact top-10 that approximate mode
returns; latency is the sample median.

| Endpoint | HNSW candidates | Coverage: dev / sample | Approximate vs exact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Similar products (C_bge) | 200 (previous default) | 0.890 / 0.959 | 0.9 vs 7.6 ms |
| Similar products (C_bge) | **800** | **0.960 / 0.991** | **2.1 vs 7.6 ms** |
| Search (bge-small) | **200** | **1.000 / 0.991** | **4.4 vs 8.5 ms** |

HNSW itself is accurate (vector recall 0.986–0.997 at every candidate count); every miss comes from using text
alone to propose candidates. A few exact results rank low on text but high on the attributes, as for 3 of the
1,000 sample products (a garden-plant listing, for one, whose exact neighbours are visors ranked 1,041st to
11,163rd by text). Filtered search at 800 candidates: a price ceiling at the query's price (273 queries, a median
of 12,964 products allowed) keeps 0.988 coverage at 2.2 vs 3.5 ms; same brand (224 queries, a median of 6 allowed,
so scored exactly) keeps 0.999, the one miss being a swap of twin listings.

**Decision.** Both endpoints default to approximate search, with the smallest candidate count that meets the bar
on both query sets: 800 for similar products, 200 for search (`serving` in `configs/default.yaml`). `mode=exact`
stays available per request, and evaluation always ranks exactly. At 30,000 products both modes are fast; the
default matters more as the catalogue grows. Index: HNSW (M 32, efConstruction 200, efSearch 128), 53 MB, built in
6–10 s (8.2 s for the current artifacts).

## D09 Text representation
*22 Oct · Decided* · evidence: `reports/text_model_comparison.json` (`python -m scripts.compare_text_models`; CPU, Apple silicon)

| | TF-IDF | all-MiniLM-L6-v2 | bge-small-en-v1.5 | all-mpnet-base-v2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dimensions / parameters | 36,372 sparse | 384 / 22.7 M | 384 / 33.4 M | 768 / 109.5 M |
| Encode 30,000 products | – | 26 s | 36 s | 133 s |
| Encode one query (median / p95) | – | 4.4 / 5.7 ms | 7.5 / 9.0 ms | 17.2 / 24.3 ms |
| Top-10 text neighbours sharing a category | 64.7% | 73.4% | 74.7% | 71.7% |

**Decision.** mpnet is dropped: four times slower and no better on this catalogue, which answers v2's
"why not a larger model". MiniLM and bge-small both stay in the labelling pool: their top-10 lists overlap
only 47.5%, and judging both costs 11% more labels. The final choice comes from the labels. TF-IDF stays
the baseline. Models are pinned to Hugging Face commits.

**Choice from the development labels** (`reports/dev_evaluation.json`, rule in D07). bge-small serves both
endpoints. Similar products, nDCG@10 over 20 queries: C_bge 0.771, C_minilm 0.761, B_tfidf 0.718, A_structured
0.504; C_bge and C_minilm are indistinguishable (difference 0.010, SE 0.023), and C_bge is chosen because search
needs bge-small anyway (D07 amendment). These are the values over all 814 development judgements: labelling the
variants (D04, D06) revealed more strong matches, which lowered every absolute score by about 0.01 while the
differences between systems moved by at most 0.006. Search, nDCG@10 over the 8 queries that have a relevant product:
bge-small 0.747, TF-IDF 0.601, MiniLM 0.564; bge-small beats MiniLM by 0.183 (95% interval 0.065 to 0.328) and
TF-IDF by 0.146 (−0.008 to 0.303), so the TF-IDF margin is not yet established on 8 queries.

**bge-small versus MiniLM overall.** Quality favours bge-small: +0.183 nDCG@10 on search (95% interval 0.065 to
0.328) and +0.010 on similar products (SE 0.023, indistinguishable). Cost favours MiniLM: query encoding 1.3 vs
3.1 ms (one thread), ONNX model 90 vs 133 MB, offline encoding of the catalogue 30 vs 57 s. Serving bge-small for
both endpoints therefore costs about 1.9 ms per search and 43 MB, in exchange for the only quality difference the
labels can measure, and keeps one model, one vector set and one index. The holdout repeats both comparisons on
unseen queries (D07).

**Runtime.** The timings above were measured with PyTorch. Since D17 the models run on ONNX Runtime, which gives
numerically near-equivalent embeddings, with six top-10 lists differing through investigated near-ties. In the
two selected configurations (PyTorch on 10 threads, ONNX Runtime on the service's single thread), query encoding
fell from 7.1 to 3.1 ms for bge-small and from 3.6 to 1.3 ms for MiniLM; this is not an equal-thread benchmark.

**Notes.** Both models are general-domain English models trained on web, question-answer and retrieval
pairs, not on fashion data; Indian garment terms such as "kurti" or "saree" may be weakly represented,
and the labels will show whether that matters. bge-small adds a retrieval instruction to short search
queries. The dataset has no description field, so product text is the title plus keyword tokens the title
does not already contain.

## D10 Semantic search
*22 Oct · Decided*

**Filter parsing: rules, not an LLM.** Deterministic, free, unit-tested (36 tests), works offline and needs
no API key for reviewers. It handles strict ("under") versus inclusive ("up to") limits, ranges, "around"
(±20%), "1k" and number words ("under a hundred bucks"). It ignores numbers that are not prices: amounts
followed by units ("5 years", "pack of 3") and amounts under 50 without a currency ("girls under 10").
*Rejected:* an LLM parser (per-query cost and latency, non-deterministic, needs a key) and a hybrid,
deferred as future work.

**Filter first, then rank.** An explicit price limit is strict and also excludes products without a price
(it cannot be verified). Audience and colour exclude only confident conflicts; unknown values stay
eligible, because hard filters on unknown values would discard most products (57% have no extracted colour).

**Ranking.** Query–product cosine plus small bonuses that reorder close matches but cannot lift unrelated
products: item colour 0.06 > "available in" colour 0.03, material 0.04, size 0.02, and a cheaper or pricier
position within the product's own category 0.05 when the query asks for it. There is no size field, so
size is only a soft title match.

## D11 Currency
*22 Oct · Decided*

Amounts in queries are taken as written in rupees (the catalogue comes from amazon.in). "under $100"
therefore means ₹100 and returns gloves and vests: a documented limitation. Converting dollars at a fixed
rate was considered and deliberately not adopted. The development labels confirm the cost: for "comfortable
running shoes under $100" and "cotton kurti for women under a hundred bucks" no system's top-10 holds a single
relevant product, so both are left out of the search means and listed in the report.

## D12 Relevance cutoff for semantic search
*22 Oct · Open*

`num_results` is a maximum: fewer results, or none, may be returned when the remaining items are clearly
unrelated. The cutoff will be chosen from development labels, not as an arbitrary cosine value, and tested
both for rejecting unrelated items and for keeping useful ones. `/find_similar_products` keeps returning
the requested count whenever enough eligible products exist. In the development labels 2 of the 10 search
queries have no relevant product at all (D11); for them the right answer is an empty list.

## D13 Catalogue limitations
*22 Oct · Decided*

The catalogue holds only 17 footwear products (2 running shoes) and no real wallets: the two "wallet"
titles are tie-and-cufflink gift sets. Results for such queries reflect the catalogue, not ranking errors,
and are reported as such.

## D14 Images
*21 Oct · Decided* · evidence: `reports/image_download_summary.json`

Only catalogue URLs are downloaded (HTTPS from Amazon's image host; redirects allowed only within Amazon's
image CDN), at most 5 MB and 40 million pixels each, three attempts, resumable, resized to ≤ 320 px.
29,861 of 30,000 are usable (137 return HTTP 404; 2 products have no URL). Images are never committed or
shipped, only derived vectors. Image features (ResNet-50) are an experiment after the essentials, and the
missing-image rule will be fixed before any weight tuning.

## D15 Semantic IDs
*21 Oct · Decided (experiment)*

Industry use is verified: Google's TIGER (arXiv 2305.05065), YouTube ranking (2306.08121), Meta ads
ranking (2504.02137) and Kuaishou's OneRec (2502.18965, 2506.13695). All of them combine the codes with
user-behaviour data, which this dataset lacks. We therefore test semantic IDs as a coarse-to-fine
candidate index over content only (structurally a multi-level IVF index), built with residual k-means as
in OneRec, and do not claim to reproduce a generative recommender.

## D16 Engineering choices
*21–22 Oct · Decided*

- **Python 3.12** with pinned dependencies; the sample Dockerfile's Python 3.10 reaches end-of-life in October 2026.
- **Offline preparation, online serving:** images, embeddings and indexes are built offline; the service
  loads them at startup and never downloads or builds anything per request.
- **Colima** (open source, no licence fees) for Docker and **kind** for local Kubernetes.
- **Repository:** public `FairosMirza/sap-cxii-tech-ex-01`, with the original repository kept as `upstream`
  and pushing to it disabled. Large artifacts stay out of Git (release asset and zip instead).
- **Dependencies in three tiers:** `requirements.txt` (serving), `requirements-build.txt` (+ scikit-learn, to
  build artifacts) and `requirements-dev.txt` (+ PyTorch and tooling). Neither serving nor building needs PyTorch.
- **Docker:** two stages. The build stage unpacks the dataset and runs `scripts.prepare` from a clean state, so a
  fresh clone needs only `docker build`; the runtime stage holds serving dependencies, data and artifacts, runs as
  a non-root user and has a readiness health check.

## D17 Inference runtime: ONNX Runtime instead of PyTorch
*22 Oct · Decided* · evidence: `reports/encoder_parity.json` (`python -m scripts.check_encoder_parity`),
smoke tests (`python -m scripts.smoke_api`), `tests/test_integration.py`

**Problem.** The first real `/semantic_search` request crashed the service (segmentation fault, exit 139).
Reproduced outside the API: the macOS pip wheels used here (faiss-cpu 1.15.1, torch 2.14.1) each bundle their own
OpenMP runtime (`libomp.dylib`), both load into one process in either import order, and PyTorch inference crashes
once FAISS is loaded. The same crash hit `scripts.prepare` whenever the embedding cache was empty (any fresh clone) and
the research scripts that load FAISS before encoding. The tests missed it because they use a fake encoder.

**Decision.** Products (offline) and queries (online) are encoded with ONNX Runtime, running each model
repository's own ONNX export at the pinned commit. The tested ONNX Runtime builds (1.31.0 for macOS arm64 and
Linux arm64) did not introduce a second OpenMP runtime: the macOS libraries link none, and in both environments
the running service maps only FAISS's. The rest of the
sentence-transformers pipeline is reproduced from the model's own files: `tokenizer.json`, truncation at 128
tokens, the bge-small query prefix, pooling read from `1_Pooling/config.json` (CLS for bge-small, mean for
MiniLM) and L2 normalisation. Any other module stack or pooling mode is rejected, not approximated. Revisions
are now pinned in code; before, the commit was only recorded after downloading the latest one. PyTorch is a
development dependency only (the parity reference and image experiments).

**Rejected.** Relying on import order (both runtimes still load; it only survived by chance);
`KMP_DUPLICATE_LIB_OK=TRUE` (OpenMP's own error message calls it unsafe and unsupported); patching installed
libraries; `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` (avoids the trigger, not the cause; single-threads offline encoding; keeps
PyTorch in the image); dropping FAISS (loses approximate search, D08).

**Verification** (Apple silicon, CPU; the previous encoder ran unchanged in a child process):

| Check | bge-small | MiniLM |
|---|---|---|
| Token IDs, all 30,000 product texts (7 cut at 128 tokens) | identical | identical |
| Product embeddings: min cosine / max element difference | 0.9999997 / 5.6e-7 | 0.9999997 / 3.3e-7 |
| Search-query embeddings: min cosine | 0.99999988 | 0.99999988 |
| Exact top-10 identical, 20 development products | 20 of 20 (C_bge) | 18 of 20 (C_minilm) |
| Exact top-10 identical, 10 development searches | 8 of 10 | 8 of 10 |
| Query encoding, median / p95 (PyTorch 10 threads → ONNX Runtime 1 thread) | 7.06 / 8.69 → 3.13 / 3.75 ms | 3.59 / 4.07 → 1.26 / 1.70 ms |
| Encoding all 30,000 products (offline) | 36.0 → 56.8 s | 19.2 → 29.6 s |

The query timings compare the two selected configurations, PyTorch on its default 10 threads and ONNX Runtime on
the single thread the service uses. They are not an equal-thread runtime benchmark: PyTorch on one thread was not
measured, and ONNX Runtime on all cores took 1.98 ms (bge-small) and 0.86 ms (MiniLM). The check ran three times:
every parity and ranking result was identical each time, while timings varied (query encoding 2.1–2.8 times faster
in these configurations, product encoding 38–60% slower); the table shows the final report.
The vectors stored before the change equal the fresh PyTorch reference exactly, so they are what was compared.

**Differences investigated.** All 6 changed lists are one swap between two listings with identical text,
whose scores differ by at most 1.2e-7. In one case ("kids winter jacket") the two are twins: same variant
family, text, rating, reviews and price, and only one can be shown. Under PyTorch their vectors differed by
1.1e-7, so floating-point noise picked the winner; under ONNX Runtime they are bit-identical and the documented
tie-break decides. Re-pooling with the new encoder retrieves 3 query–product pairs the pools lack, each a
same-text twin of a pooled pair. They form a supplemental development pool, labelled blind before evaluation
(D07); the original pools are unchanged and existing judgements stay valid for their pairs.

**Outcome.**
- Crash resolved: the live service on macOS maps exactly one OpenMP runtime (FAISS's) and no PyTorch library;
  `tests/test_integration.py` asserts this in a child process. A fresh preparation with empty caches now
